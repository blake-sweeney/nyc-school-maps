A zone gives priority, not a guaranteed seat

Living in a zone gives your child top priority at that school. Per the DOE, most zoned schools make kindergarten offers to all students living in the zone who apply on time, but a few crowded schools can’t. List your zoned school on your MySchools kindergarten application. You can rank it anywhere; your priority is the same. Each school’s “My Chances” section on MySchools shows which priority groups got offers last year.

      Before you start

      • This is an independent map made by a NYC parent. It isn’t made by, affiliated with or endorsed by NYC Public Schools.
      • Zone lines come from the DOE’s Find a School map (__ZONES_DATE__). Ratings, test scores and programs come from the DOE’s __SNAPSHOT__ School Quality Snapshot. Data can be out of date or have errors.
      • Zones change from year to year, and blocks near a boundary are easy to misread on a map.
      • Living in a zone gives your child priority at that school. Most zoned schools make kindergarten offers to all students living in the zone who apply on time, but not every school can. Applications and offers go through MySchools.

      Always confirm your exact address on schoolsearch.schools.nyc before applying.

      How full the school is: its enrollment as a share of the capacity of the rooms it uses, from the __BLUE_BOOK__ Enrollment, Capacity & Utilization Report (the “Blue Book”). Capacity assumes the city’s class size goals (20 students in K–3, 23 in grades 4–8, 25 in high school), so a school over 100% has larger classes or fewer spare rooms. Crowded schools are more likely to have waitlists, even for zoned families, and to give up art or science rooms for classrooms. Many are crowded because they’re popular.
